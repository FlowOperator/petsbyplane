Importing your pet to the UK
Bringing a pet into the UK works differently from exporting — it's a UK customs process, not a per-country lookup.
Covered arrival airports
London Heathrow London Gatwick Manchester
How it works
On arrival, your pet is collected by the airport's Animal Reception Centre (ARC), given a health check, microchip scan, and paperwork check. Once cleared, a registered clearing agent — required by law, not optional — arranges customs clearance and reunites you with your pet. The whole process is typically 4–8 hours from landing, depending on airport traffic.
What document you need, by origin
Coming from an EU country
Any one of: EU Pet Passport, UK-issued Animal Health Certificate (valid 4 months), or GB Pet Health Certificate.
Coming from a listed (non-EU) country
GB Pet Health Certificate.
Coming from an unlisted country
GB Pet Health Certificate, plus a Rabies Neutralising Antibody Titre Test (RNATT) completed at least 3 months before travel.
Health certificate requirements
• ISO-compatible microchip
• Rabies vaccination at least 21 days before arrival
• Worming treatment within 5 days (but not within 24 hours) of arrival — dogs only
• RNATT blood test if travelling from an unlisted country, at least 3 months before travel
Travel documents need to reach the clearing agent 72 hours before departure for pre-checking — late documents incur extra fees.
Common questions
Why is a clearing agent mandatory?
Arrival fees and HMRC customs clearance can only be arranged through a registered clearing agent — it isn't optional.
How long does customs take?
Roughly 4–8 hours from landing, depending on how busy the airport is.
Can my pet arrive anywhere in the UK?
Only Heathrow, Gatwick, and Manchester are currently covered; commercial imports must go through one of the London airports.
What if documents go missing or something's wrong?
It's rare after pre-checking, but the ARC can hold a pet for up to 48 hours (extra fee) while replacement documents are sorted.
