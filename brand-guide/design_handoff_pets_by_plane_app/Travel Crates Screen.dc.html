Travel crates
Getting the size right matters — it's your pet's space for the whole journey, and it drives the cost too since airline charges are based on crate volume.
Wooden crate
Best for: larger/heavier pets, made-to-measure fit
Built to your pet's exact measurements, which helps keep volume (and cost) down. Very sturdy, often required for bigger or stronger animals, and enclosed sides can reduce noise and visual stress in transit.
Plastic crate
Best for: most standard journeys
Durable, lightweight, easy to clean and reuse. Comes in standard sizes — convenient, but can mean a slightly oversized fit, and since cost is volume-based an oversized standard crate can end up pricier than a custom wooden one.
The 4 measurements we ask for
These are the ones you'll be asked for at quote stage.
Length — nose to base of tail (not including the tail)
Height (standing, natural position) — floor to top of head or ear tips, whichever is higher
Width — widest point, usually across the shoulders
Height (elbow) — ground to elbow joint
Helping your pet settle in
Crates can be delivered ahead of travel so your pet has time to get comfortable, especially useful for nervous pets or long journeys.
• Leave the crate somewhere they can explore freely, with a favourite toy or blanket inside
• Prop the door open for the first few days so they can come and go as they please
• Once they're resting in it, close the door for short periods and build up gradually
• Take them on short car trips in the crate once they're settled
Common questions
Can I use my own crate?
Yes if it's IATA-compliant — but rules change over time, so a crate that was fine a few years ago might not pass now. Photos are checked before travel; if it doesn't comply, it can be modified or an alternative sourced.
How is crate size decided?
A standard formula is applied to your pet's measurements to calculate the compliant size, which also determines the airline cost since that's volume-based.
Do I keep the crate?
Yes — all provided crates belong to you and need collecting at the destination, or can be arranged for reuse/recycling if not wanted.
What's included?
Bedding, a secure water bowl and funnel, all airline and IATA compliant. Bedding is treated with a calming pheromone scent.
What can't go in the crate?
Toys or chewable items (choke hazard) — crates are x-rayed at check-in and anything flagged causes delay. Cameras and tracking devices aren't permitted either.
