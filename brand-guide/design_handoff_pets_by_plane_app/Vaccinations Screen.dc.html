Vaccinations & tests
Requirements vary a lot by destination — some countries need only vaccinations, some need blood tests too. If your pet has no vaccination history yet, starting early matters, since several vaccines need staged doses with waiting periods in between.
Dogs
Rabies
Required almost everywhere. Not part of routine UK annual vaccination, so it's often a new one. Mandatory wait after the first dose before travel — commonly around 21 days, though some countries need much longer (New Zealand asks for 6 months). Validity usually 1–3 years depending on the vaccine.
DHPP (distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus)
A routine UK combination vaccine, also required by many destinations. One dose after 12 weeks old typically covers about three years.
Leptospirosis
Commonly required, and commonly done wrong for travel — needs two initial doses exactly 28 days apart, with the annual booster due within 12 months of the second dose. Missing that window can mean restarting the whole course.
Bordetella (kennel cough)
Not required for travel generally, but recommended for mandatory-quarantine destinations, and mandatory if boarding pre-departure (at least 14 days before boarding).
Cats
Vaccine naming varies by manufacturer, which can make this look more complicated than it is.
Rabies
Same pattern as dogs — required almost everywhere, mandatory wait after first dose, validity varies by vaccine and destination.
FVRCP
Feline viral rhinotracheitis, calicivirus, panleukopenia. Two initial doses 3–4 weeks apart. Some versions need an annual top-up between the full three-year courses.
Feline leukaemia (FeLV)
Two initial doses 3–4 weeks apart, annual thereafter. Sometimes combined with FVRCP.
Blood tests & other tests
Number required varies a lot by destination — some countries need one or two, others need several.
Rabies antibody titre test (RNATT / FAVN)
Confirms the rabies vaccine actually produced immunity. Countries often require a waiting period after a passing result — roughly 6 months for Australia, 3 months for New Zealand.
Region/disease-specific tests
Some destinations test for diseases relevant to that region — leptospirosis, brucellosis, leishmaniasis, tick-borne diseases, or FeLV/FIV for cats.
General health screening
Not always mandatory, but sometimes requested — covers general organ function and blood counts.
